Develop, debug and sideload.

Apparition is a developer tool for Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV. It builds your Xcode projects onto the device, patches Swift into the running app without a rebuild, and lets you or an agent debug it live through DebugTrace. It also sideloads other people's apps, launches them with JIT, and re-signs them every week over Tailscale, with no cable and no computer on the same network.

A Mac app, for Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV.

Download for Mac How development works

Free and open source under the MIT license. Windows and Linux work too.

Three steps, and then the device runs itself

  1. Install Apparition on your computer

    Download the app and pair your device. On Vision Pro, open Settings > General > Remote Devices and type the code it shows; Apple TV pairs the same way from Settings > Remotes and Devices > Remote App and Devices. An iPhone or iPad pairs over a USB cable once, unless it already syncs with your Mac over Wi-Fi. Then sign in with your Apple ID, or choose your own certificate and profile, and install.

    Prefer a terminal? The apparition command line does the same, and the app can put it on your PATH.

  2. It arrives ready to sign

    The computer hands Apparition on the device its pairing and the signing it used, so there's nothing to set up twice, and the two don't each take one of a free account's certificates.

  3. Install apps, or build your own

    Add a source and install from it, right on the device, or add an Xcode project and build it onto the device. A daily Shortcuts automation re-signs your apps before they expire, and launching with JIT works from the device too. Both reach your computer, left in the menu bar, only when the Apple ID work needs it.

A faster loop for your own apps

Apparition is a development tool first. Your Mac builds, the device runs, and what you change is in the running app a few seconds later. Choose Develop apps the first time the Mac app opens, or switch with the buttons in its header at any time. Both modes share the same pairing and signing.

Xcode projects to your devices
Add a project's folder and the Apparition Store, the build service inside the Mac app, reads its schemes and bundle IDs. Start a build from the Mac, or from the headset and follow it in Activity. Builds are installed and launched over Apparition's own pairing, not Xcode's, and every build is kept with a manifest of exactly what went into it. Launch a build with JIT when it needs to generate code, with its console attached.
Live patching, no rebuild
Edit a Swift file and run apparition inject: the running app has the change in a few seconds, with its state intact. You can also evaluate Swift inside the live app to read a model or try a call. The device asks you to allow code changes, so nothing is patched behind your back.
DebugTrace, built in
DebugTrace is the Swift package that makes your app debuggable while it runs, and it is an integral part of this project. Register an endpoint once and the app serves it over HTTP and MCP, so you, or an agent working on the Mac, can read its state, drive it and watch its privacy-labelled log, with no logging added and no rebuild.
Tune by feel
A value you are adjusting, such as a brightness, a curve or a timing, becomes a slider in the Mac app's Debug tab. Beside it each running dev build has a live log, a profiler that names the hot functions and a memory view.
Crashes and traces
Apps that link DebugTrace upload signed debug traces to the Store, with the breadcrumbs from the run that died. It symbolicates crash reports from the exact build installed on each device.
From a PC, through the Mac
Pair a Windows or Linux computer, or another Mac, with your Mac for developer access. Its agents drive your apps' debug servers through the Mac, and its builds go to your devices with apparition deploy, at home or over Tailscale.

Sideloading and JIT, without the weekly chore

The same tool installs other people's apps. A free Apple ID signs them for seven days, and Apparition on the device re-signs them before they expire.

Sources you already use
Add AltStore and SideStore sources, or point at a GitHub repo for apps that only publish release files, like UTM's visionOS build. Share an IPA or .deb to Apparition, and it names the app and asks before installing.
Refresh with Shortcuts
The Refresh Apps action re-signs whatever expires within three days. Run it from a daily automation, or ask Siri. Apps keep their files on the device, so a refresh needs no download and your app data stays put.
JIT that works on current chips
On iOS 26 with an A15 or M2 and later, and on visionOS 27, an app can't run generated code just because a debugger attached once. Apparition keeps the debugger attached and prepares the memory the app asks for, so emulators like UTM and ARMSX2 run, from Apparition, a shortcut or Siri.
Your own certificate
With a paid developer account, import a .p12 and provisioning profiles. Apps then sign for as long as the profile lasts, usually a year, with no sign-in and no free-account limits. A .p12 export also lets LiveContainer run any number of apps under one App ID.
One certificate for all your devices
A free account has only two certificates. Share one between your devices and computers, checked by a six-digit code both screens show or sealed under five words, and they all sign with it.
Releases you can verify
Every release is signed with a hardware key, and the apps check that signature and each download's hash before using it, wherever the file was fetched from. Releases are signed only after a dependency and reproducible-build audit.

Refresh and launch from anywhere

Put the device and the computer running Apparition on Tailscale, and the weekly refresh, signing and JIT keep working wherever the device is. No cable, no trip home, no Xcode involved and no computer on the same network.

The companion relays the connection
The device resets connections it makes to its own pairing service, so Apparition asks a companion, the Mac app left in the menu bar, to connect back to it. The device then sees the connection coming from the companion, whether that is over your Wi-Fi or over Tailscale.
It leaves the VPN slot alone
AltStore needs its computer on the same Wi-Fi. SideStore and StikDebug use LocalDevVPN, which takes the device's only VPN slot, so Tailscale can't run beside it. Apparition's companion replaces LocalDevVPN, so Tailscale stays connected.
It wakes up for you at home
With "Wake for network access" on, a sleeping Mac wakes when the device knocks. A MacBook Pro asleep answered in under nine seconds, so the Mac needn't stay awake all night to be ready in the morning. Away from home, Tailscale can't wake it, so leave it awake.
Only ciphertext crosses it
The companion carries encrypted traffic with no keys, and connects back only to the device that asked. Your Apple ID and password go straight to Apple from the device you sign in on.

Step by step setup on GitHub

Signing in: we recommend your own Mac

Since October 2026, Apple gives free-account signing only to a sign-in that carries a Mac's anisette, the data that identifies the computer. We recommend taking it from a Mac you own, as Xcode does, because no one can take it away from you, nothing about your sign-in goes to a third party and it behaves exactly as Apple's own tools. It is a recommendation, not a requirement: other sources still work, and we just don't supply one.

A Mac on macOS 15 or earlier: the best
The Mac's own anisette needs no setup and no change to its security, with SIP and AMFI staying on, and your other Apple devices get the usual sign-in push, with SMS offered as well. This is the tested setup. Intel Macs never get macOS 27, so an old Intel MacBook that stays awake makes a good always-on companion.
macOS 26 or 27: a small VM
On macOS 27, macOS keeps its anisette to itself (a real Mac on macOS 26 hasn't been tested). On an Apple-silicon Mac, Apparition makes a small macOS 26 virtual machine for it: one button, no typing and no administrator password, and it costs no security on the Mac you use. The limit: a VM's sign-ins confirm by SMS only, never by a push on your devices, where a real Mac's get the push and SMS too.
macOS 27 with SIP and AMFI off
Should work on real hardware too, and real hardware should get the device push because it doesn't present as a VM, though we haven't tested it. macOS 27 only gives its anisette with System Integrity Protection and AMFI turned off, which lowers the Mac's security. Apparition doesn't do it for you and we don't recommend it on a Mac you use for anything else, where macOS 15 keeps both on. We haven't tested whether macOS 26 needs it too.
A third-party anisette server
Still works, on any computer with no Mac at all. Give the address of any anisette v3 server, yours or someone else's, and use Check server to see what it serves. Apparition doesn't provide or recommend one, so you trust whoever runs it, and Apple decides what it accepts: a server built from another platform's libraries may be refused.
A paid developer account needs no anisette at all

A certificate and profile you made on Apple's developer website sign apps with no sign-in, so they need no anisette from anyone, and no Mac.

Works with any computer, or none

Installing, refreshing, launching with JIT and the companion run on macOS, Windows and Linux. On iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro, LocalDevVPN can stand in for the companion, with no computer at all (it takes the device's only VPN slot, so not alongside Tailscale). A computer is needed once, to install Apparition. Windows and Linux computers, and devices away from home, can also pair with a Mac running Apparition and sign in through its anisette over an encrypted channel.

Building your own apps, without a Mac

Building with Xcode and live patching need a Mac. xtool builds on Linux, and on Windows through WSL, and apparition deploy installs the result from the PC.

Requirements and honest limits

What do I need?

An Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad or Apple TV, and a computer to install Apparition. To sign apps you need an Apple ID (a spare free one is best) or your own developer certificate, and for a free Apple ID an anisette source: a Mac you own is recommended, or a third-party server. Building your own apps needs Xcode on a Mac.

Which macOS does it need?

Any Mac can run the app. For signing in with a free Apple ID, a Mac on macOS 15 or earlier supplies its own anisette with no setup, and that is the tested setup. On macOS 27 it works only with SIP and AMFI off, protections you shouldn't turn off on the Mac you use, so on Apple silicon Apparition sets up a small macOS 26 virtual machine for it (Companion > Set up the anisette VM) and runs it behind the companion. It saves itself to disk when idle, so it costs memory only around sign-ins. A real Mac on macOS 26 hasn't been tested yet. The macOS licence allows macOS VMs only on Apple hardware.

Can I use a third-party anisette server instead?

Yes. In Settings, or the sign-in's Anisette data, give a server's address. Apparition supports it on every computer, but doesn't supply an address or recommend a server. You are trusting its operator with the data that identifies your sign-in, and Apple changes what it accepts without notice. Servers that serve a Mac's anisette get through; ones built from another platform's libraries may be refused (-80046). A Mac you own is the setup we can support.

What is DebugTrace, and do I have to use it?

It's a separate open-source Swift package, DebugTrace, that you link into an app you build. It adds a logger with privacy labels, debug traces and a debug server for you and your agents. Apparition installs and refreshes any app without it. The live debugging, tuning and trace features of the Mac app need it.

Is live patching safe?

The device asks "Allow code changes?" for each patch, unless you tapped "Allow for 15 Minutes", and a refused patch changes nothing. Connecting to your Mac doesn't grant debug access either: the device approves each new client, and each screen capture, separately.

Which apps work?

Apparition signs any IPA, and any .deb package that holds an app, such as Kodi's. Whether an app then runs depends on the app. We keep a list of the apps we've run ourselves (UTM, ARMSX2, Madeira, Kodi, Provenance and more) with what we found, including the ones that need a caveat. If one you want fails, tell us.

What are the limits of a free Apple ID?

Apple allows 3 sideloaded apps per device, 10 App IDs per week and two certificates on a free account, and apps signed with it have to be re-signed every 7 days. That's the reason Apparition refreshes them. When the 3-app limit is reached, Apparition shows what's installed and lets you remove one. A paid developer certificate lifts these limits for as long as its profile lasts.

What is anisette, and what does it see?

Signing in to Apple needs "anisette" data that identifies the computer. Apparition takes it from your own Mac, or from a server you give, and runs no Apple code made for another platform. Windows, Linux and devices pair with a Mac over an encrypted channel that carries only anisette requests: your Apple ID and password go straight to Apple from the computer or device you sign in on.

Signing in fails with error -80045. What now?

Since 12 October 2026 Apple refuses sign-ins whose anisette doesn't match the device they claim to be, which broke most sideloaders. Apparition claims what its anisette really is, so it handles it. The first sign-in from a computer or device Apple hasn't seen asks for a 2FA code: Apple sends it as a push to your other devices, with SMS offered as well, when the anisette is a real Mac's, and by SMS only to your trusted phone number when it comes from a VM.

My Apple ID uses security keys. Can I still use it?

Not for signing. Create a separate free Apple ID for sideloading and sign in with that.

Does it work away from home?

Yes, JIT included, over Tailscale. Put the device and the computer running Apparition on the same tailnet, then in Apparition's Setup type its Tailscale address with port 49160 (100.x.y.z:49160). That computer at home then keeps refreshing your apps, and launching them with JIT, wherever you are. AltStore and SideStore need the same Wi-Fi or LocalDevVPN for that. Step by step.

Will my apps refresh on their own?

Apparition asks the system for background time, but that isn't dependable. A daily Shortcuts automation is: pick a time you usually use the device, add Refresh Apps, and turn on Remember password. When a refresh can't run, the action says why.

macOS says Apparition can't be opened.

It isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks it until you clear the download quarantine. Move Apparition to Applications, then run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Apparition.app

Or open it once, then click Open Anyway in System Settings > Privacy & Security. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning for the same reason: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Is it on the App Store?

No. Apparition is sideloaded, open source under the MIT license, and not affiliated with Apple.
