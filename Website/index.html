Sideloading that lives on the device itself.

Apparition installs apps from AltStore sources, signs them right on your Apple device with your own Apple ID, and keeps them refreshed. Set it up from a computer once. After that, it refreshes through a computer left running at home, reachable from anywhere over Tailscale, or with no computer at all through LocalDevVPN.

For Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV.

Three steps, and only the first needs a computer

  1. Install it once from a computer

    Download the Apparition desktop app for macOS, Windows or Linux and pair your device. On Vision Pro, open Settings > General > Remote Devices and type the code it shows; Apple TV pairs the same way from Settings > Remotes and Devices > Remote App and Devices, and shows a second code for Apparition. An iPhone or iPad pairs over a USB cable once, unless it already syncs with your Mac over Wi-Fi. Then sign in and install.

    Prefer a terminal? The apparition command line does the same.

  2. It arrives ready to sign

    The desktop app hands Apparition the pairing and the signing it used, so there's nothing to set up twice, and the two don't each take one of a free account's certificates.

  3. Install apps, and let them refresh

    Add a source and install from it, right on the device. A daily Shortcuts automation re-signs your apps before they expire. Refreshing, and launching apps with JIT, go through the desktop app left running as Apparition's companion, which doesn't need the same Wi-Fi, or through the free LocalDevVPN app on the device.

What it does

Sources you already use
Add AltStore and SideStore sources, or point at a GitHub repo for apps that only publish release files, like UTM's visionOS build.
Signing on the device
Apps are signed on the device with your own Apple ID; a free one works. Your password goes only to Apple, and can stay in the device's Keychain so refreshes run without you.
Refresh with Shortcuts
The Refresh Apps action re-signs whatever expires within three days. Run it from a daily automation, or ask Siri. Apps keep their files on the device, so a refresh needs no download and your app data stays put.
Launch with JIT
Open apps like UTM with JIT, prepared by the debugger the way Xcode does it: from Apparition, a shortcut, or Siri.
Your own certificate
With a paid developer account, import a .p12 and provisioning profiles. Apps then sign for as long as the profile lasts, usually a year, with no sign-in and no free-account limits.
A companion on your computer
Left running in the menu bar or tray, the desktop app relays the device's connection in place of LocalDevVPN, carrying only encrypted traffic, and offers it anisette. Put the device on Tailscale and a companion at home keeps signing, refreshing and JIT working from anywhere.
One certificate for all your devices
A free account has only two certificates. Share one between your devices and computers, sealed under five words, and they all sign with it.

Requirements and honest limits

What do I need?

An Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad or Apple TV, and a macOS, Windows or Linux computer for the first install. To sign apps you need an Apple ID (a spare free one is best) or your own developer certificate. Refreshing and launching with JIT need the companion or LocalDevVPN.

Which apps work?

Apparition signs any IPA, and any .deb package that holds an app, such as Kodi's. Whether an app then runs depends on the app. We keep a list of the apps we've run ourselves (UTM, ARMSX2, Madeira, Kodi, Provenance and more) with what we found, including the ones that need a caveat. If one you want fails, tell us.

What are the limits of a free Apple ID?

Apple allows 3 sideloaded apps per device, 10 App IDs per week and two certificates on a free account, and apps signed with it have to be re-signed every 7 days. That's the reason Apparition refreshes them. When the 3-app limit is reached, Apparition shows what's installed and lets you remove one. A paid developer certificate lifts these limits for as long as its profile lasts.

What is an anisette server, and which one should I use?

Signing in to Apple needs "anisette" data that identifies the device. On macOS and Linux the desktop app makes its own, and offers it to your devices as their companion; there's nothing to set up. On Windows, use another of your computers running Apparition, or any anisette server you trust, or host one with the Docker Compose file in the repository. A server sees the anisette identity and IP address, never your Apple ID or password.

Signing in fails with error -80045. What now?

Since 12 October 2026 Apple refuses sign-ins whose anisette doesn't match the device they claim to be, which broke most sideloaders. Apparition handles it. The first sign-in from a computer or device Apple hasn't seen asks for the code Apple sends to your other devices; after that, no code is needed. Accounts with no trusted devices, only a phone number for codes, can't sign in this way yet.

My Apple ID uses security keys. Can I still use it?

Not for signing. Create a separate free Apple ID for sideloading and sign in with that.

Does it work away from home?

Yes, JIT included, over Tailscale. Put the device and the computer running the desktop app on the same tailnet, then in Apparition's Setup type the computer's Tailscale address with port 49160 (100.x.y.z:49160) and use its anisette. The computer at home then keeps signing and refreshing your apps, and launching them with JIT, wherever you are. AltStore and SideStore need the same Wi-Fi or LocalDevVPN for that. Step by step.

Will my apps refresh on their own?

Apparition asks the system for background time, but that isn't dependable. A daily Shortcuts automation is: pick a time you usually use the device, add Refresh Apps, and turn on Remember password. When a refresh can't run, the action says why.

Which devices does it work on?

Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV, each with its own build. The desktop app picks the right one for the device you pair.

macOS says the desktop app can't be opened.

It isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks it until you clear the download quarantine. Move Apparition to Applications, then run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Apparition.app

Or open it once, then click Open Anyway in System Settings > Privacy & Security. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning for the same reason: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Is it on the App Store?

No. Apparition is sideloaded, open source under the MIT license, and not affiliated with Apple.
