Sideloading, run from your Mac.

Apparition keeps the apps on your Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV signed, refreshed and running, and launches them with JIT, with no cable and no weekly trip back to your desk. Your Mac does the part nothing else can: it signs you in to Apple and stays in the menu bar as the device's companion, at home or over Tailscale. It builds your own Xcode projects too.

A Mac app, for Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad and Apple TV.

Download for Mac Why a Mac?

Free and open source under the MIT license. Windows and Linux work too, paired with a Mac.

The Mac does what nothing else can

Since October 2026, Apple gives free-account signing only to a sign-in that carries a Mac's anisette. So Apparition gets it from macOS itself, on a Mac you own, the way Xcode does. Nothing leaves your network, and no third party is involved.

It signs you in
Signing in with a free Apple ID needs a Mac's anisette. On macOS 26 and earlier there is nothing to set up. On macOS 27, where macOS keeps it to itself, Apparition runs a small macOS virtual machine for it: one button, no typing and no administrator password.
It's the companion
Left running in the menu bar, Apparition for Mac relays the device's connection in place of LocalDevVPN, carrying only encrypted traffic. Put the device and the Mac on Tailscale and refreshing, signing and JIT keep working from anywhere, without taking the device's only VPN slot.
It wakes up for you
With "Wake for network access" on, a sleeping Mac wakes when the device knocks. A MacBook Pro asleep answered in under nine seconds, so the Mac needn't stay awake all night to be ready in the morning.
It builds your apps
The Apparition Store, the build service inside the Mac app, builds your Xcode projects, signs them, installs them over the device's own pairing and keeps every build. See Develop.

Three steps, and then the device runs itself

  1. Install Apparition on your Mac

    Download the app and pair your device. On Vision Pro, open Settings > General > Remote Devices and type the code it shows; Apple TV pairs the same way from Settings > Remotes and Devices > Remote App and Devices. An iPhone or iPad pairs over a USB cable once, unless it already syncs with your Mac over Wi-Fi. Then sign in with your Apple ID, or choose your own certificate and profile, and install.

    Prefer a terminal? The apparition command line does the same, and the app can put it on your PATH.

  2. It arrives ready to sign

    The Mac hands Apparition on the device its pairing and the signing it used, so there's nothing to set up twice, and the two don't each take one of a free account's certificates.

  3. Install apps, and leave the Mac running

    Add a source and install from it, right on the device. A daily Shortcuts automation re-signs your apps before they expire, and launching with JIT works from the device too. Both reach your Mac, which stays in the menu bar, only when the Apple ID work needs it.

Build your own apps, too

Choose Develop apps the first time the Mac app opens, or switch with the buttons in its header at any time. Both modes share the same pairing and signing.

Xcode projects to your devices
Add a project's folder and the Store reads its schemes and bundle IDs. Start a build from the Mac, or from the headset and follow it in Activity. Builds are installed and launched over Apparition's own pairing, not Xcode's, and kept with a manifest of exactly what went into them.
JIT and live patching
Launch a fresh build with JIT, or apply Swift changes to a running app without rebuilding, and let an agent drive your apps' debug servers through the Mac.
Crashes and traces
Apps that link DebugTrace upload signed debug traces to the Store, which symbolicates their crash reports from the build that is installed on each device.
From a PC, through the Mac
Pair a Windows or Linux computer, or another Mac, with your Mac for developer access. Its builds go to your devices through the Mac with apparition deploy, at home or over Tailscale.

And everything a sideloader should do

Sources you already use
Add AltStore and SideStore sources, or point at a GitHub repo for apps that only publish release files, like UTM's visionOS build. Share an IPA or .deb to Apparition, and it names the app and asks before installing.
Refresh with Shortcuts
The Refresh Apps action re-signs whatever expires within three days. Run it from a daily automation, or ask Siri. Apps keep their files on the device, so a refresh needs no download and your app data stays put.
JIT that works on current chips
On iOS 26 with an A15 or M2 and later, and on visionOS 27, an app can't run generated code just because a debugger attached once. Apparition keeps the debugger attached and prepares the memory the app asks for, so emulators like UTM and ARMSX2 run, from Apparition, a shortcut or Siri.
Your own certificate
With a paid developer account, import a .p12 and provisioning profiles. Apps then sign for as long as the profile lasts, usually a year, with no sign-in and no free-account limits. A .p12 export also lets LiveContainer run any number of apps under one App ID.
One certificate for all your devices
A free account has only two certificates. Share one between your devices and computers, checked by a six-digit code both screens show or sealed under five words, and they all sign with it.
Releases you can verify
Every release is signed with a hardware key, and the apps check that signature and each download's hash before using it, wherever the file was fetched from. Releases are signed only after a dependency and reproducible-build audit.

What works without a Mac

A Mac is the supported setup, but it isn't a wall. Only two things really need one.

Works with any computer, or none

Installing, refreshing, launching with JIT and the companion run on macOS, Windows and Linux. On iPhone, iPad and Vision Pro, LocalDevVPN can stand in for the companion, with no computer at all (it takes the device's only VPN slot, so not alongside Tailscale). A computer is needed once, to install Apparition.

Signing with a paid developer account

A certificate and profile you made on Apple's developer website sign apps with no sign-in, so they need no anisette and no Mac.

Signing with a free Apple ID, without a Mac

This is the part that needs a Mac's anisette. Pair your Windows or Linux computer, or a device away from home, with a Mac running Apparition, and it signs through that Mac. Without one, you can still start with a third-party anisette server, given by address. Apple decides whether that works: it gives developer access to a Mac's anisette, so a server built from another platform's libraries may be refused. Apparition doesn't provide or recommend one.

Building your own apps, without a Mac

Building with Xcode and live patching need a Mac. xtool builds on Linux, and on Windows through WSL, and apparition deploy installs the result from the PC.

Requirements and honest limits

What do I need?

An Apple Vision Pro, iPhone, iPad or Apple TV, and a Mac you own, at home or reached over Tailscale. To sign apps you need an Apple ID (a spare free one is best) or your own developer certificate. Building your own apps needs Xcode on the Mac.

Which macOS does it need?

Any Mac can run the app. For signing in with a free Apple ID, a Mac on macOS 26 or earlier supplies its own anisette with no setup. On macOS 27 it can't without turning off protections you shouldn't turn off on the Mac you use, so on Apple silicon Apparition sets up a small macOS 26 virtual machine for it (Companion > Set up the anisette VM) and runs it behind the companion. It saves itself to disk when idle, so it costs memory only around sign-ins. The macOS licence allows macOS VMs only on Apple hardware, which is one more reason the Mac has to be yours.

Which apps work?

Apparition signs any IPA, and any .deb package that holds an app, such as Kodi's. Whether an app then runs depends on the app. We keep a list of the apps we've run ourselves (UTM, ARMSX2, Madeira, Kodi, Provenance and more) with what we found, including the ones that need a caveat. If one you want fails, tell us.

What are the limits of a free Apple ID?

Apple allows 3 sideloaded apps per device, 10 App IDs per week and two certificates on a free account, and apps signed with it have to be re-signed every 7 days. That's the reason Apparition refreshes them. When the 3-app limit is reached, Apparition shows what's installed and lets you remove one. A paid developer certificate lifts these limits for as long as its profile lasts.

What is anisette, and what does it see?

Signing in to Apple needs "anisette" data that identifies the computer. Apparition takes it from your own Mac and runs no Apple code made for another platform. Windows, Linux and devices pair with that Mac over an encrypted channel that carries only anisette requests: your Apple ID and password go straight to Apple from the computer or device you sign in on.

Signing in fails with error -80045. What now?

Since 12 October 2026 Apple refuses sign-ins whose anisette doesn't match the device they claim to be, which broke most sideloaders. Apparition claims what its anisette really is, so it handles it. The first sign-in from a computer or device Apple hasn't seen asks for the code Apple sends to your other devices; after that, no code is needed. Accounts with no trusted devices, only a phone number for codes, can't sign in this way yet.

My Apple ID uses security keys. Can I still use it?

Not for signing. Create a separate free Apple ID for sideloading and sign in with that.

Does it work away from home?

Yes, JIT included, over Tailscale. Put the device and the Mac running Apparition on the same tailnet, then in Apparition's Setup type the Mac's Tailscale address with port 49160 (100.x.y.z:49160) and use its anisette. The Mac at home then keeps signing and refreshing your apps, and launching them with JIT, wherever you are. AltStore and SideStore need the same Wi-Fi or LocalDevVPN for that. Step by step.

Will my apps refresh on their own?

Apparition asks the system for background time, but that isn't dependable. A daily Shortcuts automation is: pick a time you usually use the device, add Refresh Apps, and turn on Remember password. When a refresh can't run, the action says why.

macOS says Apparition can't be opened.

It isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks it until you clear the download quarantine. Move Apparition to Applications, then run this in Terminal:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Apparition.app

Or open it once, then click Open Anyway in System Settings > Privacy & Security. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning for the same reason: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Is it on the App Store?

No. Apparition is sideloaded, open source under the MIT license, and not affiliated with Apple.
