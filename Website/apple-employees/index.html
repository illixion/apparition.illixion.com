To the people who build Apple's platforms

From Ixion, who maintains Apparition. I'm a Latvian citizen living in Latvia, and I bought my Vision Pro in Germany. I'm writing to people who work at Apple, and to anyone who wants the case laid out.

I'm not writing to get you to quit. The choices in front of you are narrower than the public story suggests, and the Vision Pro case is one of the clearest.

What the EU gives iPhone and iPad users, and Vision Pro users don't get

Since iOS 17.4, users in the European Union can install apps from alternative app marketplaces, and from developers' own websites, on iPhone and iPad. Apple's support page states the condition: the country or region of your Apple Account must be an EU country, and you must physically be in the EU. Where the device was bought is not part of the test, and nothing in the rule mentions citizenship.

Apple's support page lists alternative marketplaces for "iPhone and iPad" only. The developer page describes the same scope for "iOS and iPadOS". On Vision Pro, I can't install from an alternative marketplace or from a developer's website, even though I live in an EU country and meet the account and location conditions that Apple applies to iPhone and iPad.

Vision Pro did get something. Apple added alternative payment options for visionOS apps sold in the EU, from visionOS 1.2 in 2024. That is a payment change. Distribution, which decides what can run on the device, was left out.

Why it matters to me

Apparition is one of the projects that let people install and keep apps current on a device they own. Its users are not trying to pirate anything. They want a tool that works, and many of them would use it on Vision Pro if the platform allowed it.

The rules are also a cost to the people who maintain these tools. On a free developer account, an app expires after seven days and has to be reinstalled. Each change to the install path can break a tool that people rely on, and maintainers have to drop their own work to catch up.

FB24190179, reported against the first visionOS 27 developer beta, is a platform bug on the same device. In Clock, pin the app to a wall, leave the room, come back and reopen Clock: the app disappears until you force-quit it. I reported it through Feedback Assistant on 6 August 2026. As of 3 November 2026 it has had no response.

What Apple could do

  • Bring alternative distribution to visionOS in the EU. Apple already runs a notarized, signed install path on iPhone and iPad with EU-specific terms. Extending it to Vision Pro is a product decision, not a new technology problem.
  • Make the eligibility rules work for open-source projects. Apple's August 2026 terms widened who can run a marketplace or web distribution, but the criteria are: a moderate Dun & Bradstreet score, public listing, venture funding from an established firm, a licensed audit, a government, educational or nonprofit fee waiver, a USD 1,000,000 letter of credit, or one million first annual installs worldwide. A free project without a fee waiver or a million installs has no route in. The 5% Core Technology Commission that starts on 1 October 2026 is a smaller problem than that gate.
  • Document the install path for developers. A supported, documented way to put your own build on your own device, with clear limits, would remove most of the reason for workarounds.
  • Fix the platform bugs that block people's own tools. FB24190179 is small on its own, but many small bugs like it decide how much developers trust a platform beyond the App Store catalogue.

About the people who work here

This part is about you, not the company. Apple employees are not the people who set this policy, and I am not asking anyone to risk their job.

Retail workers at Apple have organized too. The Towson, Maryland store voted to unionize in June 2022 with the IAM union, and its first contract was ratified in July 2024, per the CWA. The Oklahoma City store voted 56 to 32 to unionize with the CWA in October 2022. In the United States, organizing is a protected right under the National Labor Relations Act, and the NLRB publishes the status of each case. For your own rights, the NLRB and an employment lawyer are better sources than this page.

What I'm not asking for

  • Confidential information, source code, internal documents, or anything covered by an agreement you signed. Please don't share them.
  • Any action that would put your job at risk.
  • Agreement on the rest of the company's business. Only the Vision Pro install path and the platform bugs are on the table here.
